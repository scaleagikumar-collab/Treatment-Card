Watching

For the nights the doctor asks you to check every hour or two.

01A timer that counts to the next reading, and tells you when one is late
02Temperature, oxygen, pulse, breathing, blood pressure
03Anything past your doctor's limit is flagged and takes you straight to the danger signs
04Symptoms and side effects recorded with how bad they were

Medicines

The long, unglamorous part that matters most.

01Copy the prescription in once — times, days, special instructions
02Mark each dose given, missed or vomited
03A missed dose asks what got in the way, and nothing scolds you for it
04What is still due today, at the top of the first screen

Your own protocols

When the doctor gives you a routine, save it once instead of remembering it at 2am.

01Name it, set how often, choose what to measure
02Add things to confirm each round — the line site, passing urine, drinking
03It records who told you and when, and asks you to check it is still current
04One tap to run it. It schedules what to watch — never a dose, never a decision

01A few days ahead it reminds you to cook and freeze meals, stock groceries, change the bedsheets

Privacy

It stays on your phone

A child's illness is nobody else's business. This was built so there is nothing to leak.

No account, no server

Nothing is uploaded. There is no login, no email, no profile. The record lives in this one browser, on this one phone.

You choose every time it leaves

Sharing happens only when you press share. Backups are a file you save yourself and can pass to another caregiver.

Quiet on the lock screen

Calendar reminders say “Care reminder” by default. Your child's name appears only if you decide it should.

Being straight with you

What this card does not do

Anything that claims to do these things for a child in treatment is worth walking away from.

  • It does not diagnose anything, and it does not read blood counts or lab reports. Those are copied in by you, for your own reference, and stay exactly as you typed them.
  • It has no thresholds of its own. Every number it reacts to is one your doctor gave you and you entered.
  • It never tells you to wait. When something crosses a limit, it takes you to the danger signs and your hospital's number — it does not put a form in front of you first.
  • It does not replace your treating team. It is a notebook that adds up, so you arrive at the visit with the facts instead of a blur.
Getting started

Three minutes, once

Set it up on the day you have the discharge summary or prescription in front of you.

Open it and add the basics

Your child's name, the hospital, the emergency number they gave you, and the day treatment started.

Copy in the instructions

The prescription, and the numbers your doctor said to call about. Never guess a dose — copy it exactly.

Add it to your home screen

It then opens like an app and works without a signal. Save a backup file whenever you want a second copy.

Early version

Tell me what is wrong with it

This is being built with families who are in the middle of treatment, and it is not finished. If something is confusing, missing, or gets something wrong that your team told you — that is the most useful thing you can send.

Especially worth telling me:

  • Anything a doctor or nurse said that this card contradicts
  • A word or screen that was hard to understand at 3am
  • Something you needed to write down and could not
  • Anything that felt like it was judging you

Please do not send your child's records. Nothing in this card is uploaded, and feedback does not need any of it — just tell me what happened in your own words.

What it does
Open the card

Treatment Card gives no medical advice and makes no clinical decisions. It stores instructions given by your child's treating team, alongside observations recorded by you at home, and presents them back to you. Readings kept in it are taken by parents, not by clinical staff. If you are worried about your child, contact your treating team or go to the hospital — do not wait for anything in this card.
